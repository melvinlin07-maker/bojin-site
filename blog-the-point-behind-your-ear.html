Bojin Behind the Ear: No Required Drainage Point

By Yu-Ting Lan, from Taiwan · 6 min read

You finish a cheek practice and remember a phrase from another routine: do not forget the point behind the ear. Now you are searching with a fingertip, wondering whether the tender place is the right one. You can stop searching. You do not need to find a sore spot to complete self-care.

This guide does not establish a required “drainage checkpoint” behind the ear. It explains why that description is incomplete, why tenderness is not a target, and what to do if the area draws your attention because of pain, swelling, or a lump. It does not teach an ear or neck massage technique.

A location in a lesson is not a drainage switch

A demonstration may use the ear as a visible landmark. A landmark helps describe where something is; it does not establish what happens beneath the skin or show that the area needs to be pressed.

The image of facial areas as roads all funneling through one checkpoint is too simple to use as an explanation for a cosmetic routine. This page does not show that a Bojin pass clears that checkpoint, carries away tension, or stops fluid from returning later.

Clinical manual lymphatic drainage is a distinct technique. Cleveland Clinic advises asking a healthcare provider before attempting self-drainage. Its clinical uses do not prove that a Bojin routine needs ear-area or neck contact.

You can leave this area out

There is no mandatory ear-area finish here. Do not add three or four passes behind the ear, press a spot until it changes, or continue down the neck just because a routine feels unfinished.

A change in facial appearance later in the day cannot tell you that skipping this area caused fluid to resettle. You do not need to repeat the session to test that explanation. For a practical ending, read how to end a Bojin session.

No point to hunt for

A tender spot is not confirmation of the right placement. If the area hurts or feels unfamiliar, lift away rather than trying to work out what it means through more pressure.

If you notice tenderness

Leave a painful or tender area alone. Do not assume it holds more tension because it is near the ear, and do not treat lighter pressure as a reason to keep working it.

There is more than one possible cause of discomfort around an ear. This article cannot tell whether it comes from the skin, a nearby structure, an ear problem, or something else. Seek medical advice if pain persists, worsens, recurs, has no clear explanation, or concerns you.

For a sensation that begins during contact, see sensations during Bojin. That guide does not diagnose the cause either.

If you notice a lump or swelling

Do not try to clear a lump with a tool, drain it yourself, or repeatedly press it to see whether it moves or becomes less tender. Its appearance and location do not let this page identify it as a knot, gland, or another structure.

Arrange medical advice for an unexplained lump, especially if it grows, persists, feels hard or fixed, or appears with other symptoms. The NHS advises assessment for concerning swollen glands and cautions against self-diagnosis. That guidance does not mean every ear-area lump is a swollen gland.

Ear-area symptoms that need prompt assessment

Get urgent medical assessment for painful swelling or redness behind the ear, particularly with fever, ear discharge, hearing changes, or an ear pushed outward. The NHS lists these among symptoms that can occur with mastoiditis, an infection that needs prompt treatment.

These symptoms do not establish that diagnosis, and the list is not exhaustive. Their presence is a reason to seek care, not to wait for a massage to help. If you are unsure how urgently to be seen, contact a healthcare service for guidance.

If the instruction itself is unclear

Put the tool down and review the whole demonstration. Ask which surface is in contact, what placement is intended, and whether the lesson is appropriate for you. Do not reconstruct a neck routine from a phrase, a diagram, or the photograph above.

The photograph shows a classroom practice setting. It is not a location map for an ear-area point. Read how to interpret a tool-angle demonstration and routine order for the learning questions, rather than using sensation to find a hidden target.

No need to finish through discomfort. Keep tools away from the ear canal and skip painful, swollen, injured, or irritated areas. Bojin is optional self-care, not diagnosis or treatment for ear symptoms, lumps, or swelling.

Quick answers

Do I have to work behind the ear to finish Bojin?

No required ear-area finish is established in this guide. You can stop a session without adding contact behind the ear or strokes down the neck. Do not continue an uncomfortable practice to complete a supposed drainage route.

Is the point behind the ear a drainage checkpoint?

This guide does not establish a single point that must be cleared for all facial areas to drain. Clinical lymphatic drainage is a distinct technique, and its uses do not validate the claim that a Bojin pass opens a checkpoint or preserves results.

Does tenderness mean I found held tension?

No. Tenderness cannot identify its cause or show that an area needs tool work. Lift away and leave a tender spot alone instead of repeatedly pressing it. Persistent, worsening, unexplained, or recurring pain needs medical advice.

Should I massage a lump behind my ear?

Do not use a tool or repeated pressure to try to clear an unexplained lump. A lump cannot be identified as a knot or a lymph node from this article. Arrange medical advice, especially if it grows, persists, feels hard or fixed, or comes with other symptoms.

When do ear-area symptoms need urgent help?

Get urgent medical assessment for painful swelling or redness behind the ear, particularly with fever, ear discharge, hearing changes, or an ear pushed outward. This is not a diagnosis, but it is a reason to seek care rather than massage the area.
