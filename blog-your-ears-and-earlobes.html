Your Ears and Earlobes: The Zone Almost Everyone Skips

By Yu-Ting Lan, from Taiwan · 6 min read

Put a finger just in front of your ear, where your jaw hinges, and open and close your mouth slowly. Feel that movement? Now press very gently and hold. For a lot of women, that's the first time they've noticed that area at all, and quite a few of them are surprised by how tender it is.

Why the area around your ears holds so much

Three things converge here. The muscle that closes your jaw runs right in front of your ear, so every clench you do during the day lands beside it. The band of tissue that runs from your temple down past your ear tightens along with the forehead. And the drainage route that carries everything downward off your face runs right behind and below the ear before it heads down the neck.

Then there are the earlobes themselves, which almost nobody thinks about. Lobes lose their firmness with age like the rest of the face, and decades of earrings, especially heavier ones, stretch the piercing over time. That's a structural change, and I'll be straight with you about what can and can't be done for it.

What bojin reaches here, and what it doesn't

The area in front of and behind your ear responds well to gentle work, because that's soft tissue and muscle you can actually reach. Easing the band in front of the ear often helps a jaw that's been braced all day, and the point behind and below the ear is where almost every session should end anyway, because it opens the route down the neck.

The key idea

The soft tissue in front of and behind your ear is reachable and worth working. A stretched earlobe piercing is a structural change, and nothing gentle and external reverses that.

The earlobe itself is a different matter. A stretched piercing hole or a lobe that's lost volume is a structural change in the tissue, and nothing gentle and external is going to reverse it. Gentle work can make the lobe and the skin around it feel softer and look a little less tired for a while, in the same temporary way the rest of your face responds. Anything more than that would need a professional, and I'd rather say so than sell you a five-minute fix.

The 5 steps for the ear zone

01

Add slip and warm the side of the face.

Flat, gliding passes from the cheek out toward the ear, warming the whole side before you work anywhere specific.

02

Find the hinge.

Just in front of the ear, where you felt the jaw move. Small, slow passes with the rounded tip, comfortable pressure, never pressing into the ear canal itself.

03

Follow the band up toward the temple.

Slow passes from just above the ear up toward the temple, where forehead tension and jaw tension meet.

04

Work behind and below the ear.

Gentle downward passes on the soft area behind the earlobe, the top of the route down the neck.

05

Finish down the neck.

From behind the ear, straight down the side of the neck, several slow passes. This is the whole point of having warmed the area.

Take the earrings out first. It's obvious once you've tried it with them in.

Honest expectations, and a safety note

You'll likely notice the side of your face feels less braced, and the jaw hinge feels easier to open. Stretched earlobe piercings and lost lobe volume will not change, and I don't want you working harder at it hoping they will.

Self-care, not medical care. This is gentle self-care, not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Never insert anything into the ear canal, and keep pressure off the bony part of the ear itself. Skip this area entirely if you have an ear infection, a recent piercing, ear pain, dizziness, or any swollen or tender lymph node behind the ear, and see a doctor about swelling or pain that persists.

Tonight, before your usual routine, spend thirty seconds just in front of your ear with two fingertips. If it's tender, you've found something worth coming back to.

Quick answers

Why is the area in front of my ear so tender?

The muscle that closes your jaw runs right in front of the ear, so daytime clenching lands there, and the band running down from the temple tightens along with the forehead.

Can bojin fix a stretched earlobe piercing?

No. A stretched piercing hole is a structural change in the tissue, and nothing gentle and external will reverse it. That needs a professional.

Is it safe to work near my ears?

Keep pressure off the bony part of the ear and never insert anything into the ear canal. Skip the area entirely with an ear infection, ear pain, dizziness, or a swollen lymph node.

Should I take my earrings out first?

Yes. The tool needs a clear path around the lobe and behind the ear, and earrings get in the way.
