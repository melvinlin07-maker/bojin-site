Who Should Skip Bojin for Now (or Ask a Doctor First)

By Yu-Ting Lan · 6 min read

You found a method you want to try. You watched a few videos, you have a smooth stone in a drawer somewhere, and you're ready. So here I am, the person who teaches this, telling you that some of you should put the stick down and wait.

I mean that. This is one of the few pages on this whole site that isn't trying to get you started today. If you're on certain medications, healing from something, or just not sure what's going on with your skin, the kind thing I can do is draw a line before I hand you a technique.

I always tell my students the same thing. Read your own state first. The face in the mirror this morning is the only one that matters, and some mornings the answer is "not today."

Why a face method needs an honest "wait" list

Bojin works on the soft tissue of your face with steady, comfortable pressure. On a calm, healthy face, that feels good. On a face that's inflamed, freshly treated, or bruising easily, that same pressure lands on tissue that needs to be left alone.

Nothing here is dramatic. It's the same common sense you'd use before a firm massage or a warm bath after surgery. When skin is doing repair work or reacting to something, adding pressure gets in the way.

So the list below isn't me scaring you off. It's me telling you what I'd tell a friend before she picked up the stick.

Recent Botox or filler

If you've had Botox or dermal filler recently, give it time before you work over those areas. Pressure and movement can shift settling product, and that's a conversation for the person who did your injections, not for me.

The plain reason to wait: you paid for a specific result, and you don't want to nudge it around while it's still settling. Your injector can tell you how many days or weeks to hold off. That's what "ask your professional first" means here. You ask the person who treated you, and you follow their number, not a stranger's.

I wrote a whole separate piece on this because so many women ask. See what to wait for after Botox or filler for the longer version.

Blood thinners, or skin that bruises very easily

If you take a blood thinner, or you've always been someone who bruises from the smallest bump, that's a real reason to check in before you start.

The comfortable pressure I teach is gentle, but "gentle for most people" and "gentle for you" can be different sentences. When your body marks up easily, the safer move is to ask your doctor whether facework makes sense for you, and if so, how light to keep it. Let them draw that line.

A rosacea flare, active breakouts, or broken skin

If your rosacea is flaring, if you're breaking out, or if the skin is broken anywhere you'd be working, wait until it calms and closes.

Running a tool over inflamed or open skin spreads the irritation and slows the settling. This one you can usually judge yourself. If the skin is red, tender, bumpy, or not intact, it's telling you it's busy. Come back when it looks and feels like your normal skin again.

A history of skin cancer, or any unexplained lump

If you have a history of skin cancer, or you feel a lump, a firm spot, or a change you can't explain, don't work over it and don't try to figure it out with a stick.

I'm not qualified to tell you what a lump is, and neither is any video. This is the clearest "ask your doctor first" on the list. Get it looked at by a professional, and let them tell you what's safe to touch.

Recent facial surgery, a laser, or a peel

If you've had facial surgery, a laser, a peel, or any procedure recently, that skin is in the middle of its own timeline. Adding pressure over healing tissue isn't self-care, it's interference.

Your surgeon or provider knows your timeline. Ask them when the area is fully settled and cleared for normal touch. Until you have that answer, leave it be.

Pregnancy, or any condition where you're just not sure

If you're pregnant, or you live with a health condition and you're honestly not certain whether this is a good idea, treat that uncertainty as your answer for now.

Uncertainty isn't a small thing to push past. Bring it to your doctor, describe what the method involves (steady, comfortable pressure on the face with a smooth tool), and let them tell you yes, not yet, or here's how to keep it light. You lose nothing by asking first.

And the front of the neck, for everyone

One spot I ask everyone to stay off, not just the people on this list: the front and center of the neck, the throat area. It's a sensitive, delicate part of the body, and there's no comfort or benefit in pressing there.

When we work the neck at all, we keep to the sides and stay soft. Think of the throat as simply off the map. That's caution and comfort, not some special forbidden zone.

If you're clear to begin, begin gently

Say none of the above is you. Your skin is calm, you're not healing from anything, and you have no reason to wonder. Wonderful. You still start small.

Begin with the smooth gua sha stone you already own, the jade or rose quartz one in your drawer. You don't need to buy anything to try this. Use light, comfortable pressure and short sessions, and notice how your skin feels the next morning.

The pressure you're after isn't featherlight and isn't forceful. It's enough to meet the tension under the surface and never enough to hurt. If a spot stings, you're too heavy; if you feel nothing at all, you're too light. When you're ready for a tool built for this, a stainless steel bojin stick is easy to clean, durable, and made with no animal material.

Honest expectations, and one plain safety line

Here's the honest part. Even when everything is clear and you're doing it right, bojin gives you gentle, temporary results that build with consistency. It isn't an overnight fix, and it was never meant to replace anything your doctor does.

This is gentle self-care, not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If you have a medical condition, take medication, or are healing from any procedure, check with a professional before you begin. That's the whole rule, and it's the same one I follow with my own students here in Taiwan.

Before you pick up the stick

If any line on this page sounded like you, that's not a door closing. It's a reason to ask one good question first. And if you're new here, start with is bojin safe and what is bojin to see how the whole method fits together.

Quick answers

Can I do bojin if I take a daily blood thinner?

Ask your doctor first. Blood thinners can make skin mark up more easily, so the safer path is to let your own provider tell you whether facework fits you and how light to keep it.

How long should I wait after Botox or filler?

There's no single number, and it depends on what you had and where. Ask the person who did your injections and follow their timeline. Our Botox and filler piece covers what to watch for.

My rosacea comes and goes. Can I still try this?

Wait for a calm window. When your skin is flaring, red, or tender, leave it alone and come back once it settles and feels like your normal skin again.

Is it safe to work on the front of my neck?

Stay off the front and center of the neck, the throat area. When you work the neck, keep to the sides and stay soft. It's about comfort and caution.
