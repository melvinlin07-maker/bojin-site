Peach Fuzz, Facial Hair, and Dermaplaning: Does Bojin Change Anything?

By Yu-Ting Lan, from Taiwan · 6 min read

Two questions arrive in my inbox more often than you'd guess, usually written carefully, sometimes with an apology attached. The first: does working my face with a tool make the fine hair on my cheeks worse? The second, from the women who've started dermaplaning: how long should I wait afterward before I do my routine?

Neither question deserves an apology. Both deserve a straight answer.

Where the fuzz came from in the first place

The soft, fine hair on your cheeks and jaw has a name, vellus hair, and most of us have had it our whole lives without noticing. What changes after 45 or so is hormonal. As estrogen drops through perimenopause and after, some women find that fine hair along the jaw and upper lip becomes coarser and more visible, while the rest of the face's hair stays as it was.

That shift is happening at the follicle, driven by hormones. It has nothing to do with what you do on the surface of your skin, which is exactly why the first question has a reassuring answer.

What a tool on your skin does and doesn't do to hair

Working your face with a bojin stick does not make hair grow back thicker, darker, or faster. Neither does massage, neither does a gua sha stone, and neither does a washcloth. That belief comes from shaving, where a blunt cut end feels coarser than a tapered tip, and even there the hair itself hasn't changed. Bojin doesn't cut anything at all, so there's nothing to blunt.

So if you have peach fuzz and you've been avoiding facial work because you were worried about encouraging it, you can stop worrying. Your routine and your fuzz are unrelated.

The key idea

Bojin doesn't cut hair, so there's nothing to blunt and nothing to make grow back coarser. Your routine and your peach fuzz are unrelated.

Dermaplaning is a different conversation, because that genuinely does something to your skin surface: a blade removes the fine hair and the top layer of dead skin with it. Afterward the skin is smoother, more receptive, and more exposed than usual. That's the real consideration, not the hair.

How to fit the two together

Here's the practical version. On a normal day, fuzz or no fuzz, the method doesn't change at all: same order, same angle, same comfortable pressure. Your tool glides over fine hair without catching, especially with enough slip, and a stainless steel bojin stick with a little oil moves over it easily.

After dermaplaning, give your skin a rest. The surface has just been exfoliated, and adding a tool to freshly bared skin is asking for irritation you don't need. Most women find waiting two or three days is comfortable, and if your skin still looks pink, wait longer. Your own practitioner's advice always leads on timing.

The 5 steps for a normal fuzz day

  1. Use enough slip. A little more oil or cream than you might for bare skin, so the tool glides over fine hair rather than dragging at it.
  2. Warm the area first. Flat, gliding passes, as always, so nothing has to be forced.
  3. Work at your usual angle and comfortable pressure. No adjustment needed for fine hair. Nothing about it changes the method.
  4. If you feel any tugging, add slip rather than pressure. Tugging means the surface is too dry, not that you need to push harder.
  5. Finish down the neck. The same close as always.

Honest expectations, and a safety note

Bojin will not reduce facial hair, slow it down, or change its color. It doesn't touch the follicle, and any claim otherwise is a claim to be skeptical of. What it does is what it always does: a gentle, temporary easing of tension, on a face that happens to have some fine hair on it, like most faces do.

Self-care, not medical care. This is gentle self-care, not medical advice. If your facial hair has changed noticeably and quickly, that's worth mentioning to your doctor. After dermaplaning, waxing, threading, or laser, follow your practitioner's aftercare timing before adding any tool to your skin.

Your fuzz doesn't change your routine, and your routine doesn't change your fuzz. After dermaplaning, wait a couple of days and let your skin settle.

Quick answers

Does using a bojin stick make facial hair grow back thicker?

No. Bojin doesn't cut hair, and nothing you do on the skin's surface changes the follicle. The thicker-regrowth idea comes from shaving, and even there the hair hasn't changed.

How long should I wait after dermaplaning before doing bojin?

Most women find two or three days comfortable, longer if skin still looks pink. Your own practitioner's aftercare timing always leads.

Will my tool catch or tug on peach fuzz?

Not with enough slip. If you feel tugging, add more oil or cream rather than more pressure.

Can bojin reduce facial hair?

No. It doesn't reach the follicle. If your facial hair has changed noticeably and quickly, mention it to your doctor.
