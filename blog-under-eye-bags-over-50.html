Under-Eye Bags? What Causes Them and What Actually Helps

By Yu-Ting Lan, from Taiwan · 8 min read · Updated November 24, 2026

The eye-area series: brows, then lids, then under the eyes

  1. Heavy, low brows
  2. Hooded, heavy eyelids
  3. Under-eye bags (you are here)

Some mornings they’re barely there. Other mornings they’re the first thing you see. Some of what sits under your eyes is fluid that gathered overnight, and some of it is the way this area changes with age. They need different things, so it helps to know which is which.

Under-eye bags, read aloudSpoken English overview
The short answer

Under-eye bags usually come from two things: the soft fat that cushions the eye shifting forward with age, and fluid that gathers overnight. The fat is structure; no cream or home tool moves it. The fluid part changes day to day, and gentle self-care around the eye, about twice a week, can help you look a little more rested for a while. Swelling that is sudden, one-sided or painful needs a doctor. This is self-care, not medical care.

Why bags show up now, when they never used to

None of this is your fault. Behind the lower lid there’s a soft cushion of fat that protects the eye. With the years, the thin layer that holds it back loosens, and some of that fat can ease forward. The skin over it is the thinnest on your face, and it loses firmness, so the shape shows through more than it used to.

Then there’s fluid. Lie flat for seven or eight hours and some of it settles around the eyes, which is why bags often look worst first thing in the morning and soften once you’ve been upright for a while. A salty dinner, a glass of wine, a late night or a good cry can all add to it.

Habits that make mornings puffier

  • A salty or late dinner, or a drink in the evening. Many women notice their eyes the next morning.
  • Sleeping completely flat or face-down. An extra pillow keeps your head a little higher.
  • Rubbing your eyes. The skin here is thin; rubbing tugs at it and can make it puffier. Pat instead.
  • Allergy season. If your eyes itch and swell together, that’s worth raising with your doctor.

A 10-second under-eye check

  1. Morning and evening. Look in the same mirror, in the same light, when you wake and again before bed. If the bags are much softer by evening, fluid is a big part of it.
  2. Look up, keeping your head still. If the bulge under your eyes looks fuller when you look up, shifted fat is likely part of the story, and that part won’t change with self-care.

One honest note: fingertips and most home tools mostly move the top layer of skin, a millimeter or two deep. Under the eye, that layer is even thinner. That’s exactly why pressing harder never helps here.

Where to start tonight

Start above the eye, not under it. With a little cream on your fingertips, begin just above the inner end of one brow and make small, light circles along the brow bone out to the tail. Lift off and return to the start. Three slow passes, about a minute for that side, then the other. It eases the forehead and brows that weigh on the whole eye area, and it’s the same first step Yu-Ting shows in the free 1:18 demo.

Why not under the eye? That skin is the thinnest on your face, and it’s easy to press too hard or drag it. In the course, Yu-Ting teaches the under-eye area later, in order, once your pressure is right.

Honest expectations, and a safety note

Self-care can’t move fat or tighten loose skin, and it won’t erase a true bag. What many women notice is a more rested look and a calmer eye area, especially on puffy mornings, gently and for a while. For bags that are there every day, a dermatologist or eye specialist can tell you what your options are.

This is self-care, not medical care. Never press on the eyeball, keep the pressure light around the eyes, and skip broken or irritated skin. Swelling that is sudden, in one eye, or comes with redness, pain, itching or a change in vision needs a doctor.

Quick answers

What causes bags under the eyes?

Usually a mix: the soft fat that cushions the eye can shift forward as the tissue holding it back loosens with age, the skin under the eye thins, and fluid can gather there overnight. The first two are structure; the fluid part changes day to day.

Can bojin get rid of under-eye bags?

No. It can't move fat or tighten loose skin. Gentle self-care around the eye can help you look a little more rested for a while, and it's kindest to morning puffiness that changes day to day. For bags that are there all the time, ask a dermatologist or eye specialist.

Why are my eye bags worse in the morning?

Lying flat for hours lets fluid settle around the eyes, and a salty dinner, a glass of wine or a good cry the night before can add to it. It usually eases once you've been upright for a while.

Is it safe to work under my eyes at home?

Only very gently, and never on the eyeball. The skin under the eye is the thinnest on your face. That's why this guide starts above the brow, and why Yu-Ting teaches the under-eye area later, in the course, once your pressure is right.

When should I see a doctor about swelling around my eyes?

If swelling appears suddenly, affects one eye, comes with redness, pain, itching or a change in vision, or doesn't settle within a few days, see a doctor.
