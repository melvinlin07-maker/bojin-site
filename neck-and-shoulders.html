Your concern · Neck & Shoulder Line

The side of your neck, and why your face feels it

This page is about one line: the side of the neck, from just behind the ear down to the edge of the shoulder. When it stays tight, the head drifts forward, the jaw braces, and the face can look heavier.

By Yu-Ting Lan · Taiwan-based bojin instructor · Updated November 23, 2026

The short answer

Two long muscles run down the side of the neck. One runs from behind the ear to the collarbone and turns your head; the other runs from the base of the skull out to the top of the shoulder. Hours looking down at a phone or laptop keep both working. Light, slow work along the side of the neck can help them let go. Stay off the front of the neck. Pain or tingling down the arm, or neck pain after an injury, needs a doctor.

Try it tonight

One gentle line from ear to shoulder

Start in the soft hollow just behind your earlobe. With a little cream, glide your fingertips lightly down the side of your neck to the edge of your shoulder, staying behind the pulse you can feel at the front. Then turn your head slowly each way and notice the difference.

“Be careful with this one. People who aren’t sure of the spot can drift toward the carotid artery, and that can do more harm than good.”Yu-Ting Lan, on new students

Keep it light. Never press on the front of the neck. Skip it if you feel dizzy, and ask your doctor first if you take blood thinners. This line starts where the jaw line ends.

Common questions

Neck and shoulder line questions

Is it safe to work on my neck?

The side and back of the neck, with light pressure, are fine for most healthy adults. Never press on the front of the neck. Skip it if you feel dizzy or have had a recent neck injury, and ask your doctor first if you take blood thinners.

Can it get rid of neck lines?

No. Lines and loose skin come from thinner skin and loosened support, which gentle work can’t change. Some people find the neck and jaw look less pulled when the side of the neck lets go. That doesn’t last.
