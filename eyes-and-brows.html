Heavy lids, lower brows, and eyes that tire by afternoon

Brows sitting a little lower than they used to. Lids heavy by three o’clock. A tired look in photos after a full night’s sleep. Here’s what’s usually behind it, a 10-second check, and one thing to try tonight.

By Yu-Ting Lan · Taiwan-based bojin instructor · Updated November 23, 2026

The short answer

A few small muscles hold the eye area. The ring of muscle around the eye narrows into a squint, and the forehead muscle lifts the brows and helps hold heavy lids open. Screens make both work harder: we normally blink about 15 times a minute, but only about 5 to 7 times while looking at a screen, according to the American Academy of Ophthalmology. Gentle work just above the brow can help the area feel lighter. Never press on the eyelid or the eye itself. Sudden drooping, eye pain, or vision changes belong with an eye doctor.

Try it tonight

One gentle step above the brow

With a little cream on your fingertips, rest them just above the inner end of one brow. Make light circles, not too big and not too small, moving out along the brow bone to the tail of the brow. Stay on the bone, never on the lid. Then compare both brows in the mirror.

“This step isn’t hard. Most students get it right; the only difference is the size of their circles. You want them in between: not too big, not too small.”Yu-Ting Lan, on new students

Common questions

Eye and brow questions

Can bojin lift hooded eyelids?

It can’t change the skin or fat that make a lid hooded. Some people find the lid feels lighter when the brow and forehead stop holding. That doesn’t last and varies from person to person.

Is it safe to work this close to the eyes?

Yes, when you stay on the brow bone with light pressure. Never press on the eyelid or the eye. Take out contact lenses first, and ask your eye doctor before starting if you’ve had eye surgery.
