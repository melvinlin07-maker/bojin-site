Is Bojin Safe? Who Should Be Careful, and When to Skip It

By Yu-Ting Lan, from Taiwan · 5 min read · Updated October 14, 2026

Before you try anything new on your own face, it is fair to ask: is this suitable for me? A short, comfortable self-care routine is the aim here, but gentle does not mean risk-free. Your skin, medication, and any recent procedure matter. This guide helps you decide when to pause and ask a professional.

The short answer

Bojin is presented here as hands-on facial self-care, not as treatment for a health condition. Start only on comfortable, intact skin, with clean hands, a smooth tool, and a product your skin already tolerates. Fingertips are one option for learning contact, but they can still apply too much pressure.

Pressure, placement, skin condition, and knowing when to stop all matter. A routine should not hurt or leave bruising. Following a demonstration does not replace individual advice when you have a skin condition, take medication that affects bruising, or have had a recent procedure.

When to wait, or check with a professional first

Pause in the situations below. Do not try to make an unsuitable session safe merely by pressing more lightly.

  1. Broken, irritated, or sunburned skin Work around anything that's cut, raw, sunburned, or breaking out, not over it. A cold sore, an active blemish, or a fresh scratch wants to be left alone until it has healed.
  2. A recent procedure, injection, or peel If you've had Botox, filler, a peel, laser, or any cosmetic treatment, ask the professional who did it when it's fine to resume and how gentle to be near the treated area. Give it time and let them guide you. I wrote the fuller version in what to wait for after Botox or filler.
  3. Rosacea or skin that is inflamed or easily irritated Do not massage through a flare. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends avoiding rubbing, scrubbing, or massaging rosacea-prone skin. If you have eczema, active acne, or another skin condition, ask your dermatologist whether hands-on face care fits your care plan.
  4. You bruise easily or take blood thinners Ask your clinician before adding pressure-based face care. Do not assume light pressure makes it suitable. Unexplained bruising is also a reason to get advice.
  5. Anything that feels medical, not cosmetic A new lump, swelling, numbness, pain, or something that appeared suddenly or sits on only one side of your face is not something to work on at the mirror. That's a reason to see your doctor, not to reach for a tool.
  6. Pregnancy, or an ongoing health condition If you are unsure whether hands-on face care is appropriate, ask the clinician who knows your health. This guide does not establish safety for pregnancy or a particular condition.
The key idea

Safety in bojin lives in the pressure and the pauses, not in force. When something feels off, the right move is always the same: ease up, or stop, and check with a professional if you're unsure.

How to keep every session kind

Once you know the few times to wait, the rest is easy. These are the small habits that keep bojin comfortable, whether it's your first week or your hundredth.

  1. Always use a little slip A few drops of facial oil or a layer of moisturizer let the tool or your fingers glide instead of dragging the skin. Never work on a dry, bare face.
  2. Keep the pressure comfortable Firm enough to feel, never enough to hurt. If you're wincing or holding your breath, you've gone too hard. A comfortable pressure, kept up over time, does more than one hard session.
  3. Stay off the eyeball, and go softest around the eyes The skin around the eyes is the most delicate on your face. Work the bone and the brow, never the eyeball itself, and use the gentlest touch you have there.
  4. Keep your tool and hands clean Wash your hands and wipe your tool before and after. Stainless steel is easy to keep clean, which is part of why the method uses it.
  5. Stay off the front and center of the neck When you work the neck at all, keep to the sides and the back, and stay soft. The throat area is simply off the map. That's comfort and caution, nothing more.
  6. Keep it short, and stop if anything hurts A calm few minutes is plenty. Pain is a signal, not a goal. If a spot feels sore, that's your face asking you to ease off, not to push through.

What can I expect?

You may find a short session relaxing, but there is no guaranteed appearance change. Comfort afterward is a useful check. Pain, bruising, persistent redness, or new swelling calls for stopping; seek professional advice when a reaction persists or concerns you.

And the real limit: this is a gentle wellness habit, not medical care, and it can't diagnose or fix a health concern. It's wonderful for the everyday tension we all carry in a face that talks, squints, and clenches through a long day. It is not the place to sort out anything painful, sudden, or one-sided. That belongs with a doctor.

Do this kindly. Keep the pressure comfortable and let the angle of the bone do the work. Never force it or drag the skin. Skip any broken, irritated, or sore skin, stay off the eyeball, and stop if anything hurts. This is gentle self-care, not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If you have a skin or health condition, a recent procedure, or anything that seems off, check with a professional first.

You do not have to start today. If you are unsure about a symptom, medication, or procedure, pausing is a sensible choice. If hands-on care is appropriate for you, follow one short demonstration without forcing pressure or trying to produce a visible reaction.

Once you're sure it's safe for you, the next thing worth getting right is exactly how hard to press, since comfortable pressure is what keeps every session kind. Quick answers

Can I do bojin every day?

The beginner plan on this site uses two or three short sessions a week. This is a teaching arrangement, not a clinical safety limit. Do not practice on irritated or tender skin, and follow individual advice where needed. See the frequency guide.

Is bojin safe if I've had Botox or filler?

Ask the professional who performed your procedure before massaging a treated area. Aftercare differs by procedure, product, and your situation; this page does not give a universal waiting period. Read the questions to ask after Botox or filler.

Can bojin cause bruising?

Pressure-based face care can cause bruising, especially when contact is too forceful or you bruise easily. Bruising is not a desired result. Stop, and ask a clinician if you take blood thinners, bruise unexpectedly, or are concerned.

Is bojin a medical treatment?

No. Bojin is gentle beauty and self-care, not medical care, and it isn't a treatment for any condition. If you have a skin or health concern, a recent procedure, or anything on your face that appears suddenly, sits on one side, or comes with pain, please see your doctor rather than working on it at home.

Where these precautions come from

NCCIH's massage safety guidance describes risks and advises discussing suitability with a health professional. It concerns massage broadly, not this Bojin routine. The rosacea advice above comes from the American Academy of Dermatology. Neither source establishes Bojin's safety or cosmetic results.
